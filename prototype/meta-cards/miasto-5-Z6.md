## Before signatures, merchants signed with a mark — and you just read a wall of them.

A **gmerk** was a merchant's personal sign, used in place of a signature in an age when most people couldn't write. It went on cloth bales, barrels, crates and contracts — proof of who owned what, no letters required.

The "Alley of Gmerks" you searched is actually a modern installation: the marks were set into the pavement during a recent street renovation. In 1454 they lived where they belonged — carved into the **Artus Court**, the merchants' own great hall.

Toruń sat inside the **Hanseatic League**, a web of some 200 trading towns, and grew rich on the *staple right*: every passing merchant had to lay his goods out for sale here first. That wealth would later bankroll a thirteen-year war.

You were reading the closest thing the Middle Ages had to a brand.
