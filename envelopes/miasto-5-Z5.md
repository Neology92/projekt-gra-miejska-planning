## TEXT — Z5 (player reads this)

---

*A baker's boy finds you in the carnival crush, flour still grey on his sleeve, and half-shouts his master's need over the drums: hands, and now.*

*Master Bogumił's workshop is all heat and haste — feast-orders for the great houses due by morning, and a careless apprentice has wiped the labels from the spice jars and set the lids back wrong. The baker will not heat the oven on a guess; one wrong root sours the whole batch.*

> I cannot tell one grey dust from another by eye, and I've no hour to spare — but you've sharp noses, and you're from here, so you'll know them. Smell them, weigh them in the hand, shake them for what stirs inside. Only do not open them: a spice once aired is a spice spoiled. And hold to my recipe — half the names they handed me never went near a piernik.

*He works while he talks, and what he says is not what couriers of the Council like to hear:*

> Big orders tonight, while the great men feast and nurse their grievance with the castle. Let it come to war — and it will — and see who pays. The lords keep their walls and their tolls; I lose my custom, the honey doubles, the roads close on my spice. A rising of rich men, and bakers and fishwives foot the bill. Rich stay rich, poor stay poor, and I'm the last sober head in a town gone to dancing. There — now will you name my jars before the oven dies?

*You carry letters tonight for the very men he means. You say nothing of that. You take up the first jar.*

*Six jars, and no two alike — if your senses are keen enough to part them. Weigh each in the hand; shake it for what rattles, pours, or sloshes; draw its breath. Set the right name to each letter on his card. Some of the names he was given belong to no piernik at all — the recipe tells which. And where two jars are nothing but silent, scentless dust, let the recipe weigh in: what the dough wants more of will sit the heavier in the jar.*

---

> **↪ TO THE GAME MASTER** — *game instruction, not part of the world*
> Bring the filled card to the baker. If the pierniki come out right, you'll have your pay — and a sealed letter telling you what to do next.
